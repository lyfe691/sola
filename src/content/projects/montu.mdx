# Building a journal someone else can keep writing

Montu (montu.ch) is a mountain-tour journal in Swiss German. I built the site; the person it was built for writes the tours, takes the photos and publishes them himself. The tour page was not the part that took the thinking. Making every piece of the site editable without a deploy was.

## A diary, not a tour portal

The site says it itself, in the section about the project: "mehr Tagebuch als Führer", more diary than guidebook. Technical data is there as a signpost, not as the point.

The obvious build for tour content is a portal: filters, a searchable route database, sortable tables, ratings. Two published tours need none of that, and empty filters would have been the first thing on the page to look broken. So the order everywhere is photograph, then prose, then numbers. The home page lists tours as two large photo cards with a stat row underneath: elevation gain, distance, grade.

## The CMS is the whole surface

A tour is one Sanity document split into five tabs: Allgemein, Inhalt, Strecke & Karte, Logistik, Extras. The editor fills in a form, not a page.

Difficulty is a dropdown carrying the Swiss scales in full: T1 to T6 for hiking, L through EX for ski and alpine tours, WT1 to WT6 for snowshoe tours. Grades are picked, never typed, and the label the page prints comes from the same table. Each section of a route (an Etappe) carries its own photos, elevation, distance and a `skippable` flag, and the field explaining the alternative appears only once that flag is on:

The same rule holds outside tour pages. The hero images, the navigation labels, the Impressum and the Datenschutz page are Sanity fields as well. Publishing fires a webhook at an internal revalidate route, which calls `revalidatePath` for the affected page; every page also revalidates on its own hourly timer as a fallback.

## One GPX file feeds both the map and the chart

A GPX file is what a watch or phone records on the way up: a list of points with latitude, longitude and altitude. The writer uploads one per tour. The page parses it on the server with togeojson, walks the points with a haversine distance to build a cumulative distance-and-elevation series, then hands the coordinates to Mapbox and the series to a Recharts area chart through shadcn's Chart wrapper.

The chart derives its own axes: the vertical step is 50, 100, 250 or 500 m depending on the range, and the horizontal axis takes at most eight round-kilometre ticks. That is what lets a 5.5 km splitboard tour and a 28.2 km crossing from Linthal to Brigels share one component and both stay readable.

## Where it stands

Two tours are published, and the site has needed no deploy from me since they went up. For a site this small the rendering was never the hard part. The hard part is the form on the other side, and whether the person filling it in can publish the third tour without asking me anything.
