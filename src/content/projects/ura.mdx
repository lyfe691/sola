# Snowmobile navigation for Lapland

Ura is an app for snowmobile riders in Finland, on iPhone and Android. It puts every official snowmobile route in the country on one map and tells you, while you ride, whether you're on one. I designed and built it for a client, along with its website, [ura-app.com](https://ura-app.com).

A rider looks at the phone for a second at a time, at a junction, often in the dark and wearing gloves. So Ura is one map screen with one line of text at the top, and everything else opens in a sheet from the bottom.

## One answer at the top of the screen

The line at the top answers the question a rider has at a junction. On a route, it shows a green dot and the route's name. Off a route, it turns amber and shows how far away the nearest one is, with an arrow pointing back to it. Far from any route, or outside Finland, it says that instead. The screen stays on while Ura follows you, so a quick look doesn't mean unlocking the phone with gloves on.

## A verdict that holds still

A phone's GPS position drifts, more so under trees, and a plain distance check would flip between on and off route every few seconds near a junction. Ura only calls you off a route once you've been clearly away from it for ten seconds. Coming back counts straight away: the moment you're on the route again, the line turns green and names it. Where two routes run side by side, it keeps the name of the one you're on instead of swapping between them.

## Every route on one map

Zoom out and the whole network is there, from the south of Finland to the far north of Lapland. Fuel stations, restaurants and cafés, huts, shelters and fire sites near the routes are on the map as well. Tap one and a sheet shows its name, how far away it is and which way to go. Fuel, food and rest stops can each be hidden in the settings.

## Without signal

Coverage in the fells comes and goes, so Ura doesn't depend on it while you ride. The routes and places are stored in the app, and the map keeps the areas you've already looked at, so a valley you rode into with signal is still drawn after the signal is gone. When the routes change, the app fetches the new set the next time it's online.

## Theme and language

Ura has a light and a dark theme, and it follows the phone's setting unless you pick one. The dark theme darkens the map too, for riding through the long nights of a Lapland winter. The app is in English, Finnish and German, again following the phone unless you choose. The store images use the same screens, here in the dark theme, set on a photograph of snow-covered forest.

## The website

[ura-app.com](https://ura-app.com) introduces the app to riders before they install it. Its main section is the scroll story at the top of this page: one phone stays beside the text and changes to show each part of the app. The site follows the visitor's light or dark setting, a button in the corner switches it by hand, and an aurora drifts behind the headline. On a computer, a QR code at the end of the page opens the download on your phone.
