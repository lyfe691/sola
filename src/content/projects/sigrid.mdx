# A shop window, not a shop

Dulces Momentos de Sigrid is a four-page Spanish site for a home baker, my mom. It shows seventeen things she bakes, sorted into four categories, and then hands the visitor to Instagram or WhatsApp. There is no cart, no order form and no prices. That was the first decision, and the rest of the build follows from it.

The stack is ordinary on purpose: Next.js 16 with the App Router, React 19, Tailwind v4, shadcn/ui for the primitives and Motion for the animation, deployed on Vercel. Empty repo to live site took 28 commits over three days in March 2026.

## Why there is no checkout

She sells the way she has always sold: someone messages her, they agree on a cake, she bakes it. A store would mean stock counts, delivery windows, payment and refunds, which is machinery for a problem she does not have. So the site's job is narrower. Show the work, then get out of the way: `/contacto` lists Instagram, WhatsApp and email, links out, and stops. All three links are still stand-ins (instagram.com, wa.me and a placeholder address), waiting on her real accounts.

The cost is that I cannot measure any of it. No form submissions to count, no analytics on the site, so the only signal that the site did anything is her mentioning that someone found her through it.

The three featured cards are not plain images. Each one is a WebGL plane running a small fragment shader: the pointer position feeds a bulge that pushes the texture outward under the cursor and eases back to flat when you leave. It is the heaviest thing on the site, which is why it runs on three cards and nowhere else.

## The catalogue is a TypeScript file

There is no CMS. `lib/data.ts` exports two arrays, four categories and seventeen creations, and every page reads them at build time:

<CodeBlock
  code={`export type Creacion = {
  name: string
  description: string
  image: string | null

}

export const creaciones: Creacion[] = [
  {
    name: "Galletas de Mantequilla",
    description: "Clásicas y doradas, con un toque de vainilla",
    image: null,
    category: "galletas",
  },
  // …16 more
]`}

A CMS would have meant a service, an account and a login she would use twice a year. The tradeoff is real: she cannot add a cake herself. A new item means she sends me a photo and a line of text, I commit, Vercel redeploys. That works while I am the one maintaining it, and stops working the day I am not.

## Filtering without the grid jumping

`/creaciones` is the whole catalogue behind a row of pills: Todas, Galletas, Pasteles, Cupcakes, Pan Artesanal. Two Motion details carry it. The filled pill is one element with a shared `layoutId`, so it springs from button to button instead of a highlight blinking off in one place and on in another. The cards animate `layout` inside an `AnimatePresence` in `popLayout` mode, so a filtered-out card fades while the surviving cards slide into the space it left.

Tapping a card opens a lightbox rendered through a portal on `document.body`. It also calls `lenis.stop()` while it is open: the page runs on Lenis, a smooth-scroll library, and without stopping it the background keeps scrolling under the overlay.

## The photos are not real yet

Every entry in `lib/data.ts` ships `image: null`, and the components fall back to `creacion.image ?? "/placeholder.jpg"`. So the grid above is the same strawberry cake seventeen times. The hero video and the Sobre Mí photo are hers; the catalogue is waiting on one afternoon with a camera. I kept the fallback rather than hiding items without a photo: a grid of stand-ins still shows how the page will behave with real ones, and a page that hides its catalogue shows nothing.

Dark mode is the other unfinished part. next-themes runs with `defaultTheme="system"` plus a `d` hotkey, but the palette is cream and brown in both schemes, so in practice only the nav pill flips to charcoal. Either the dark theme gets a real palette or the toggle should go.
