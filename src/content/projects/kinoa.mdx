# Free films and series, and a place to watch them together

[Kinoa](https://kinoa.to) is a free site for watching films and series, with a social side built around the player: profiles, a feed, comments, messages and rooms for watching together. It started as a search box and a player, and everything else came one release at a time.

| -------------- | ------------ | ------------- |
| Around 30,000  | Around 1,000 | November 2025 |

## Finding something to watch

The home page is a single headline and a search bar, with a small white creature peeking over the bar's edge. Results arrive while you type, and the rest of the first title that starts with what you typed is completed in grey. The creature follows the caret, looks puzzled when nothing matches and tears up when the field is cleared.

A search palette opens from any page. It grows out of the search field in the sidebar and shrinks back into it when you're done. Search and a few other things work from the keyboard:

| Shortcut                   | Does                                    |
| -------------------------- | --------------------------------------- |
| `Ctrl K`, or `⌘K` on a Mac | Open the search palette from any page   |
| `↑` and `↓`                | Move through the results                |
| `Tab`                      | Accept the grey completion              |
| `Enter`                    | Open the highlighted result             |
| `Esc`                      | Close the search                        |
| `D`                        | Switch between the light and dark theme |
| `S`                        | Show or hide the sidebar                |

## Browsing by genre and language

The Discover page is for browsing when you don't have a title in mind. It opens on genre tiles, each holding three of the genre's posters that fan out when you point at it, with rows such as hidden gems and films coming to cinemas below. Picking a genre replaces the tiles with results on the same page. The filters above them live in the address (Science Fiction is `/discover?genre=878`), so Back steps through them and a shared link opens the same view. The language filter finds a language by its English name or its own, so "Korean" and "한국어" both work.

## Watching together

A room is a link. The host picks a title and shares the room, and everyone who joins watches the same moment, with a chat beside the player. When the host pauses, skips ahead or switches episode, the room follows, and a guest who falls behind gets a Catch up button. The host also runs the room:

- **Handing over.** The host can pass hosting to anyone in the room. If the host leaves, hosting passes to the next person at once, and if the host's tab closes without leaving, someone still in the room takes over after a couple of minutes.
- **Moderation.** The host can remove people. The person who started the room can also ban them from coming back, while they're hosting.
- **Visibility.** Public rooms are listed on the Watch Together page, and a private room can only be joined through its link.

## Profiles, the feed and messages

Around the player sits a small social network:

- **Profiles.** Claiming a handle gives an account a page at `kinoa.to/@name`, with a cover, a bio and tabs for its activity, favourites and watchlist. One switch makes the tabs private, while the name, bio and follower counts stay visible.
- **Comments.** Every title has threaded comments, sorted by top or new, and a comment can be marked as a spoiler.
- **The feed.** It shows new comments from everyone on Kinoa, and favourites and follows from public profiles, newest first. Signed in, a side panel adds release dates and new episodes for the titles on your watchlist.
- **Messages.** Direct messages have reactions, editing and deleting, and blocking and reporting.
- **Notifications.** A bell collects replies, mentions, follows and messages as they happen, and its Titles tab lists what's coming out from your watchlist.

## Design and motion

Kinoa's default look is a white page set into a grey frame, near-black in the dark theme, with blue kept for focus and selection. It follows the system's light or dark setting unless you pick one, and Settings adds other themes and lets you set your own colours and contrast. In the sidebar, the current page is drawn as a tab joined to the content panel, and the tab pours over to the row you pick. The rest of the motion is small and confirms what happened:

- Menus and popovers grow out of what opened them, and menus frost the page behind.
- Dialogs change size smoothly, and their buttons keep their place between steps.
- Pages stream in, and each part appears where its placeholder stood.
- A rejected form shakes the field that's wrong.

The motion steps back for visitors who ask their system for reduced motion. Kinoa also installs as an app, from the browser on a computer or Android, or with a short guide on an iPhone.

## How it's built

Kinoa is a Next.js app, mostly server components, on Supabase for accounts, the database and the realtime channels that rooms run on. It's deployed on Vercel and translated with next-intl.

### Keeping a room on one clock

The embedded players are the hard constraint: they can't be told to pause or jump to a time. So a guest who drifts away from the host reloads their player at the host's position, projected forward by how long a reload takes. Each reload measures where it actually landed and corrects the next one, and it aims a fraction of a second behind the host, since being early spoils a scene and being late doesn't.

<CodeBlock
  code={`/** Aim a touch behind the host — being early spoils, being late doesn't. */
  export const TARGET_DRIFT_SECONDS = -0.75
  /** Share of each landing error folded into the next lead estimate. */
  export const LEAD_LEARNING_RATE = 0.6
  export function anchorTargetFor(
    host: HostAnchor,
    now: number,
    leadSeconds: number
  ): AnchorTarget {
    if (!host.isPlaying) {
      return { time: Math.max(0, host.currentTime), play: false }
    }
    return {
      time: Math.max(0, projectPlayhead(host, host.at, now) + leadSeconds),
      play: true,
    }
  }
  export function nextLoadLead(lead: number, driftAtSettle: number): number {
    const error = driftAtSettle - TARGET_DRIFT_SECONDS
    const next = lead - error * LEAD_LEARNING_RATE
    return Math.min(
      MAX_LOAD_LEAD_SECONDS,
      Math.max(MIN_LOAD_LEAD_SECONDS, Number.isFinite(next) ? next : lead)
    )
  }`}
  fileName="lib/watch-together-playhead-sync.ts"
  lang="ts"
  highlight="14,19-20"
/>

Reloads cost a buffer, so the room only asks for one when it has to:

| What happens                         | When                                                            |
| ------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------- |
| The host's player sends its position | Every 5 seconds, and on every play, pause and seek              |
| Each guest measures its drift        | Every second                                                    |
| The Catch up button appears          | More than 4 seconds off the host                                |
| The guest reloads by itself          | Two checks in a row that far off, at most once every 20 seconds |
| It stops reloading by itself         | After 3 reloads that don't bring it back                        |

Rooms play on VidFast alone, because the trick needs a player that starts where it's told rather than where it last stopped.

### Matching anime episodes by air date

Everything in Kinoa, from links to watch history and comments, identifies a title by its TMDB id. The anime players take an id from AniList, an anime database, and an episode number instead, and the two catalogues cut shows differently. TMDB lists all 85 episodes of Re:Zero as one season, where AniList has an entry for each part:

| AniList entry   | Episodes | TMDB season 1 | Aired   |
| --------------- | -------- | ------------- | ------- |
| Season 1        | 25       | 1–25          | 2016    |
| Season 2        | 13       | 26–38         | 2020    |
| Season 2 Part 2 | 12       | 39–50         | 2021    |
| Season 3        | 16       | 51–66         | 2024–25 |
| Season 4        | 19       | 67–85         | 2026    |

So Kinoa looks up the AniList entries behind a TMDB id, then each entry's air dates, and pairs episodes by date, a day either way.

An episode whose date doesn't line up is paired only when it sits next to a confirmed match and both catalogues have the same number of unmatched episodes there. Otherwise it stays unpaired and plays on the regular servers, since a wrong pair plays the wrong episode and a missing one only changes the server. Across 30 popular shows I checked, this pairs 99 to 100 percent of episodes.

### Rules the database enforces

Three rules live in the database rather than the app, so they hold for any client:

- **Only the host steers a room.** It's a row-level security policy on the room's control channel. An integration test signs in as a guest, sends host commands such as kick and sync, and checks that the host receives none of them.
- **A removed guest is cut off at once.** Supabase Realtime checks a private channel's policy when a client joins and has no call that drops one user from it. So each room's channel names carry a key that changes on every kick or ban: everyone else rejoins under the new key, and the removed client is left on a channel nothing writes to.
- **Handles are claimed in one place.** One database function owns every rule, from length and reserved names to moderation and a 30-day wait between changes, and a unique index settles two people claiming the same name at once.

### Fewer round trips

Three costs ran far more often than they needed to:

| Work                                                  | Before                                                  | After                                       |
| ----------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------- |
| Checking who is signed in                             | 3 calls to Supabase Auth on a movie page, 4 on the feed | 1 per render, shared by every helper        |
| Drawing a watchlist                                   | A TMDB request for every title on it                    | One query a page: each row carries its card |
| The version check each open tab makes every 5 minutes | A serverless function each time                         | Vercel's edge cache                         |

Checking a session isn't a local decode: it goes over the network, which is why doing it once per render mattered.

### Keeping the translations complete

English is the source for every string. A check in CI, `pnpm check:i18n`, compares the keys of every other language file against it and fails when one is missing or left over.
